# Key numbers: pattern suggestions demo for a bookkeeping office

> **ALL DATA ARE SYNTHETIC.** The office 'Nordkyst Regnskab', its 180 clients, 6 bookkeepers and every posting are generated by `generate_postings.py` (seed 42). Nothing here is measured from a real accounting office or any real product. The numbers show what the method could do under the stated assumptions; they are not evidence about reality. Time per touch is an assumption, not a measurement.

Data: 99,651 postings, 180 clients (72 small service, 36 retail, 36 holding, 36 trades), Nov 2025 to Oct 2026. Patterns are found on Nov-Apr and backtested on May-Oct.

## 1. Automation today

- **Automated to some degree: 66.9 %** (suggestion_accepted 51.9 %, rule_to_journal 5.0 %, rule_posted 10.0 %); manual 33.1 %.
- **Posted without a person (rule_posted): 10.0 %.** 27 of 180 clients (15 %) have any rule.
- *Means:* two thirds of postings are prepared by the system, but only one in ten reaches the books without a person touching it. Automation stops before the finish line.
- *Rests on:* the case fact (67 % automation) and a calibration of the generator to ~10 % rule_posted and ~15 % of clients with rules. rule_to_journal exists only on bank postings. No real rule data was used.

## 4. Grouping bank texts into payees: rules vs AI

Both use `bank_text` only; `vendor_true` is the answer key. 189 unique masked texts; counterparty transfers (customer and one-off supplier payments) are out of scope.

| | Rule-based (regex) | AI (Claude Sonnet, one batch) |
|---|---|---|
| Merges (two vendors in one group, dangerous) | 1 | 0 |
| Postings on the wrong side of a merge | 161 | 0 |
| Splits (one vendor in several groups, harmless) | 36 vendors, 78 extra groups | 4 vendors, 4 extra groups |
| Completeness (postings in the vendor's largest group) | 37.9 % | 89.0 % |
| Precision (postings) | 99.74 % | 100.00 % |
| Coverage (postings given a group) | 95.0 % | 98.2 % |

- **Selected method: ai** (fewest merges, then highest precision). The only rule-based merge is GOOGLE IRELAND = Google Ads + Google Workspace.
- *Means:* a merge would make one rule wrong for two payees, so it is the dangerous error; splits only create more, smaller patterns to approve. Rules split a lot (low completeness); AI consolidates.
- *Rests on:* synthetic bank texts that are fairly recognisable (brand names, 3 to 6 spellings each). Real bank texts are messier, so the AI precision of 100 % is probably optimistic. The AI mapping is cached in `eval/ai_mapping.json` so the run can be reproduced without calling the model again.

## 6. Time saved: three scenarios, two minute models

Scenarios: **Ceiling** = every pattern with hit rate >= 95 %, all approved. **Expected** = hit rate >= 95 %, no card settlements, no special VAT, 50 % approved. **Conservative** = the same patterns, 30 % approved.
Minutes: **Touch cost equal** (default) = every touch costs the same: 0.5 / 1 / 2 min (low / mid / high). **Suggestion cheaper** = a suggestion or journal touch costs half of a manually touched posting: 0.25 / 0.5 / 1.0 min against 0.5 / 1 / 2 min. Mid is shown in bold, low to high in brackets.

| Scenario | Touches saved / month | Share of touches | Model | Hours / bookkeeper / month | Hours / office / month (north star) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ceiling (94 patterns) | 3,174 | 42.5 % | Touch cost equal | **8.8** (4.4 to 17.6) | **53** (26 to 106) |
| Ceiling (94 patterns) | 3,174 | 42.5 % | Suggestion cheaper | **5.3** (2.6 to 10.5) | **32** (16 to 63) |
| Expected (92 patterns) | 740 | 9.9 % | Touch cost equal | **2.1** (1.0 to 4.1) | **12** (6 to 25) |
| Expected (92 patterns) | 740 | 9.9 % | Suggestion cheaper | **1.2** (0.6 to 2.4) | **7** (4 to 14) |
| Conservative (92 patterns) | 444 | 5.9 % | Touch cost equal | **1.2** (0.6 to 2.5) | **7** (4 to 15) |
| Conservative (92 patterns) | 444 | 5.9 % | Suggestion cheaper | **0.7** (0.4 to 1.4) | **4** (2 to 9) |

- **Office = one accounting office (AO), 6 bookkeepers.** The north star is hours saved per office per month; per bookkeeper is the same number divided by 6.
- **Assumption:** part of the gain comes from rule_to_journal postings (a bank workflow sends them to the journal today). Counting that gain requires a bank workflow that can post directly. At mid (Touch cost equal) it is 6.5 h (ceiling), 1.7 h (expected), 1.0 h (conservative) per office per month.
- *Means:* with Touch cost equal the saving is the number of touches removed times 1 minute; with Suggestion cheaper most removed touches were already cheap suggestions, so the saving is about 40 % smaller. The case's +-0 hints at the equal model.
- *Rests on:* minutes per touch are assumed, not measured; 100 % of pattern postings that match would be booked right at 95-99 % accuracy; approval shares of 50 % and 30 % are guesses; touches saved are averaged over May-Oct.

## 7. Kill check (2 hours per bookkeeper per month)

Mean hours per bookkeeper per month at mid minutes; the 2 h line is the kill threshold.

| Scenario | Touch cost equal | Suggestion cheaper |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling | 8.8 h, above (0 of 6 bookkeepers under 2 h) | 5.3 h, above (0 of 6 bookkeepers under 2 h) |
| Expected | 2.1 h, above (2 of 6 bookkeepers under 2 h) | 1.2 h, **BELOW** (6 of 6 bookkeepers under 2 h) |
| Conservative | 1.2 h, **BELOW** (6 of 6 bookkeepers under 2 h) | 0.7 h, **BELOW** (6 of 6 bookkeepers under 2 h) |

- **Below 2 h: Expected / Suggestion cheaper, Conservative / Touch cost equal, Conservative / Suggestion cheaper.** The kill rule as defined (conservative scenario) gives **KILL** with Touch cost equal and **KILL** with Suggestion cheaper.
- **Break-even:** the expected pattern set clears 2 h per bookkeeper only if about 49 % of its patterns are approved with Touch cost equal, and about 83 % with Suggestion cheaper (the scenarios assume 50 % and 30 %).
- *Means:* at 1 minute per touch the idea is marginal: it clears the line only when roughly half of the safe patterns are approved (equal) and not at all at 30 % adoption; if suggestions are cheaper than manual touches the case is clearly below the line unless nearly every safe pattern is approved.
- *Rests on:* the same assumptions as number 6; the threshold of 2 h per bookkeeper per month is a set test, not derived. The break-even assumes saved hours scale linearly with the share of approved patterns.

## 8. What stays manual

Share of all touches, May-Oct 2026:

| Group | Share of touches |
|---|---|
| Long tail: customer and one-off supplier payments (no cross-portfolio pattern) | 38.2 % |
| special_vat: representation, EU purchases, mixed use (never in a suggestion) | 13.0 % |
| Removed if every pattern >= 95 % is approved (ceiling) | 42.5 % |
| Other: patterns under 95 %, no history, misses | 6.3 % |

- **Long tail 38.2 % + special_vat 13.0 % = 51.2 % of touches lie outside what this mechanism targets.** Over all 12 months the long tail is 38.2 %.
- *Means:* patterns remove repeated payee postings; they do not touch one-off payments or postings needing a VAT judgement, which is why the ceiling is below 50 %.
- *Rests on:* the long tail is identified from the bank text prefix (OVERF. FRA, BETALING, ...), special VAT from the generator's flag; the 'other' share is a remainder.

## 9. Value over time: the 3 safest patterns plus one rent workflow

- **Posted without a person: 10.0 % before (Nov-Apr), 13.6 % after (May-Oct average; October 13.5 %).** Touches per client per month 41.6 to 39.8.
- Approved from May: Clearhaus fees [CLEARHAUS] (14 clients, low), Teya card fees [TEYA FEES] (10 clients, low), Circle K [CIRCLE K] (8 clients, low), RENT [RENT] (1 clients, medium).
- Corrections per month after approval: 3, 2, 2, 4, 0, 4 (May to Oct).
- **Simulated exception: the rent workflow at C087 (bookkeeper B2) holds back one October payment.** Usual rent 6,427 DKK from the same payee (12 postings in 12 months); on 2026-10-27 a payment of 19,300 DKK (3.0x) to a new payee arrives. The workflow holds it for that client only; the bookkeeper chooses Update workflow, Book as usual or Keep on hold.
- *Means:* the approved workflows lift the share without a person by a few points and save about two touches per client a month; the rest of the value is in the 90 suggestions still waiting. An amount-and-payee check lets a workflow stop on one odd payment instead of booking it blindly.
- *Rests on:* a simulation. Approval in May, approved patterns auto-post every matching touched posting, corrections = actual backtest misses. The held payment is an invented overlay (not in postings.csv, not counted in any series); the amount is 3x the client's own median rent.
